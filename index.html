    Core Concept

    The Big Idea in Predictive Policing

    Shifting law enforcement from reactive responses to proactive crime prevention using historical data patterns.

    Presentation: Core Principles

    1. Reactive vs. Proactive Policing

    Traditional policing operates on reaction: a crime occurs, a call is made, and officers respond. Predictive analytics changes this paradigm. By analyzing past crime patterns, we project where and when future crimes are highly likely, allowing officers to deploy beforehand.
    Crucial Rule for Officers
    Predicts Places & Patterns, Not People!

    The system forecasts "Street block A has a high risk of theft between 9:00 PM and 11:00 PM." It does not target individuals or predict who will commit a crime.

    The 5-Step Loop:
    1. Data Collection (Historical reports)
    2. Model Training (Analyzing patterns)
    3. Forecast generation (Risk maps)
    4. Tactical Action (Directed patrols)
    5. Feedback feed-in (Refining predictions)

    Interactive training simulators demonstrating how statistical models calculate crime risk without the complex mathematics.

    Kernel Density Estimation

    Instead of looking at raw pins on a map, KDE smooths crime counts across coordinates. Placing an incident sends out a "glow" that fades with distance. Where halos overlap, a bright "hotspot" forms.

    Interactive Exercise: Click anywhere on the map grid to place a crime. Watch how the heatmap calculates and expands the glowing risk zone in real-time.

    Near-Repeat Effect

    Studies show that once a home is burglarized, that specific home and its immediate neighbors face a much higher risk of follow-up attempts for the next 2-4 weeks.

    Feedback Loop Mechanism
    Model Bias Detected!

    If you only deploy patrols to high-risk beats and leave lower-risk beats unpatrolled (0 cars), you'll only record crimes in the patrolled beats. The model will interpret this as "No crime occurred in other beats," leading to a biased feedback loop!

    Safe Patrol Policy Maintained!

    Excellent! By maintaining a minimum patrol floor of at least 1-2 cars in every single beat (including low-risk ones), you ensure random crimes elsewhere are recorded. This breaks the model bias and keeps the dataset clean.

    Key Rules for Resource Managers:

    • Minimum Patrol Floor: Never leave a beat at zero cars.
    • Weight Severity: Prioritize violent crimes over minor thefts when allocating.
    • Log Actions: Always compare patrol hours to predicted crime hotspots for audit trails.

    Real-World Case Studies
    🇺🇸 USA (PredPol):

    Failed by re-flagging already over-policed neighborhoods. It learned from drug arrest data rather than actual reported crimes, creating a systemic bias loop. Several cities discontinued it.

    🇬🇧 Kent, UK Trials:

    Conducted an honest trial. When they found the tool did not yield significant drops in violence compared to normal policing, they safely shut it down. Lesson: a negative trial is a success.

    🇳🇱 Netherlands (CAS):

    A successful model. Excludes ethnic/demographic metrics by design, passes through strict ethics boards, and serves strictly as a human advisory briefing rather than dispatch commands.

    Non-Negotiable Safeguards
    Human in the Loop: Models only brief and advise; they never auto-dispatch officers or trigger direct arrests.
    Explicit Explanations: Officers must be able to state, in clear language, exactly why a specific area was flagged.
    Target Places, Never People: Score locations and times for patrol presence, never individuals as "chronic offenders."
    Audit Log Trail: Archive every prediction along with actual patrol actions and results for external checks.
